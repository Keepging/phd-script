# Reprocessing of public proteomics data with a different pipeline: how authors claim agreement with the original study, and with which metric

**Method / access caveat (read first).** In this session every full-text host was blocked by the environment's network policy (nature.com, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, europepmc.org, ebi.ac.uk/PRIDE, biorxiv.org, pubs.acs.org, mcponline.org, api.crossref.org all returned "CONNECT 403" or DNS failure for both WebFetch and curl). Only WebSearch (extended mode) worked. All sentences marked "verbatim" below are text that the search engine extracted from the cited page; they were not verified against the full article, and section/figure attribution is given only where the extract itself stated it. Where a metric, value, threshold or sentence could not be obtained, "not found" is written and nothing is inferred. Papers were released 2015-2026 unless stated.

## Compiled list of reprocessing / cross-pipeline papers (objective table)

### Takeaway
Across 15+ papers located, no paper states a pre-declared numeric threshold for "accepting" a reanalysis as having reproduced the original study. The metrics actually used are: (a) number of identifications vs the original tool, (b) overlap of identified protein/phosphosite lists, (c) CVs of replicates, (d) Pearson correlation of protein quantities between tools or between reanalysis and original matrix, (e) overlap of differentially-expressed (DE) lists. Sign/direction concordance of log2FC and correlation of log2FC between original and reanalysis were not found in any located paper.

### Inferences
- The only located papers that quantitatively compare a reanalysis to the *original authors' published result matrix* (rather than to another tool run by the same authors) are Walzer et al. 2022 (OpenSWATH re-run vs original protein fold-change tables: overlap counts and modest DE-list overlap) and the glaDIAtor-nf paper (sample-wise Pearson r 0.47-0.76 vs original). Both explicitly report discordance rather than claiming full reproduction.
- Tool-developer papers (MaxDIA, DIA-NN, FragPipe) frame agreement as "more identifications at the same FDR" plus overlap and CV, not as reproduction of biological conclusions.
- Correlation between DIA-NN and Spectronaut protein quantities on the same raw data, when reported, is ~0.85-0.89 (Fernández-Vega) — this is a between-tool figure, not a reanalysis-vs-original figure.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports sign/direction concordance of DE proteins or a Pearson/Spearman r of log2 fold changes between original and reprocessed analyses; no paper found that declares a numeric threshold (e.g., r > x, overlap > y %) for accepting a reanalysis.
- Exact Pearson values in the MaxDIA replicate heat map, exact Fig. 1c statistics in DIA-NN 2020, and Fig. 2c counts in Demichev 2022 could not be read because full texts were inaccessible.

## Key question 1: Sinitcyn et al. 2021 (MaxDIA) vs Spectronaut on the same data

### Takeaway
MaxDIA was benchmarked against Spectronaut 13/14 on the authors' own 27-replicate HepG2 DIA dataset using protein-group counts, three-way overlap and median CV; the paper also compared protein-ratio distributions on the Bruderer "small-ratio four-species" dataset. No sentence claiming reproduction of another group's biological findings was found.

### Cited Findings
- Verbatim (Results): "To evaluate the performance of MaxDIA, we ran it, as well as Spectronaut 13 and Spectronaut 14, on a dataset comprising 27 technical replicate injections of peptides derived from the human HepG2 cell line measured in DIA as well as a DDA library created from 12 high-pH reversed-phase fractions." — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-021-00968-7)
- Verbatim (Results): "Using default parameters in both software, including a 1% FDR on precursor and protein levels, we obtained 6,238 protein groups mapped to Entrez Gene identifiers with MaxDIA compared to 6,015 with Spectronaut 13 and 6,304 with Spectronaut 14, with an overlap of 5,542 among all software platforms." — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-021-00968-7)
- Precision metric: "substantially higher quantification precision obtained with MaxLFQ in MaxDIA compared to both Spectronaut versions, with median CVs of 0.072, 0.109 and 0.114, respectively" (extract; Results). — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-021-00968-7)
- Figure legend (extract): "Heat map with all pairwise Pearson correlations among the 27 replicates for MaxDIA (upper triangle) and Spectronaut (lower traingle)" [sic]; representative replicate pairs were chosen at the median Pearson correlation. Numeric r values: not found. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-021-00968-7)
- The paper "compared protein ratio distributions on the small-ratio four-species dataset from Bruderer et al." (public dataset re-analysis); metric values: not found. The Methods state the authors "downloaded the following publicly available datasets" (list truncated in extract). — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-021-00968-7); [MPG repository PDF](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3358265_1/component/file_3364370/content)
- Own data deposited as PXD022582 (DDA) and PXD022589 (DIA). — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-021-00968-7)
- Abstract-level claim: MaxDIA "gives substantially better coefficients of variation in protein quantification than other software when using spectral libraries". — [PubMed 34239088](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34239088/)
- Independent later benchmarks: Lou et al. report "analysis of HF and TIMS data with Spectronaut and MaxDIA using different classes of libraries gave rise to higher protein/peptide quantification variation than DIA-NN and Skyline" — [Nat Commun 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35740-1); a 2024 PAC/FASP study found "the best precision value was obtained by MaxDIA (median of 0.84), but with a reduced number of identifications compared to Spectronaut and DIA-NN data" — [PMC11121386](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11121386/).

### Inferences
- MaxDIA's "agreement" framing is identification parity (within ~1-4 % of Spectronaut), a 5,542-protein three-way overlap, and lower CV; there is no claim of reproducing an external study's conclusions.

### Gaps
- Pearson r values in the replicate heat map, the exact list of downloaded public datasets (PXD IDs), and the four-species ratio-accuracy numbers: not retrievable (full text blocked).
- Conclusion sentence of the Spectronaut comparison beyond the counts sentence: not found.

## Key question 2: Demichev et al. 2020 (DIA-NN) and Demichev et al. 2022 (dia-PASEF) comparisons with Spectronaut

### Takeaway
Both papers compare DIA-NN to Spectronaut on the same raw files using identification counts (precursors/proteins at matched FDR), LFQbench ratio precision and protein-detection consistency; the 2022 paper reprocesses public dia-PASEF data (Meier et al. 2020; a 50-sample CLL study) and reports +74 % precursors / +48 % proteins over Spectronaut directDIA and 46,497 vs 26,348 precursors over OpenSWATH. No correlation of quantities or fold-changes with the original analyses was found.

## Key question 3: Large-scale reanalyses (ProteomeHD, quantms, Ochoa, Jarnuczak, Walzer/Expression Atlas) and validation against original results

### Takeaway
ProteomeHD, Ochoa 2020 and Jarnuczak 2021 reprocess hundreds-to-thousands of PRIDE raw files with MaxQuant but no validation against the original studies' results was found; quantms reports "+6 % quantified proteins vs original results" on PXD016921; Walzer et al. 2022 is the only large-scale effort that explicitly compares to original publications and finds "basic agreement at a fold-change level" with "often modest" overlap of DE sets.

### Cited Findings

- Verbatim: "To assemble ProteomeHD we processed the raw data from 5,288 individual mass-spectrometry runs into one coherent data matrix, which covers 10,323 proteins (from 9,987 genes) and 294 biological conditions"; "The 5,288 MS raw files were processed using MaxQuant 1.5.2.8."; "MS raw data were produced in-house or downloaded from the PRIDE repository". MaxQuant output deposited as PXD008888. — [PMC6901355](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6901355/); [GitHub](https://github.com/Rappsilber-Laboratory/ProteomeHD)
- Validation of reprocessed SILAC ratios against the original publications: not found.

## Key question 4: ProteomeXchange/PRIDE reanalysis (RPXD) concept and stated criteria for a "successful" reanalysis

### Takeaway
ProteomeXchange defines reprocessed datasets (RPXD) procedurally (provenance, scope, who may submit) in its 2019 guidelines; neither the guidelines nor the 2017/2020/2023/2026 consortium papers state any numeric criterion for a reanalysis to be considered successful or to have reproduced the original study.

### Cited Findings
- "Guidelines for handling ProteomeXchange reprocessed datasets", Version 1.0.2, September 2019: defines "reanalysis" as reprocessing of original raw data to reach a new result and "revision" as making the earlier instance obsolete; verbatim: "A reprocessed PX dataset (RPXD) can correspond to the reprocessing of an individual dataset, a subset of an individual dataset, or a group of datasets (complete or subset)."; two submission cases (via PX members, section 1.2.1; third parties, section 1.2.2); the `reprocessed` parameter yields an RPXD accession; "In RPXDs, the original source is always cited, with direct links to the original datasets". — [PX reprocessed guidelines PDF](https://www.proteomexchange.org/docs/reprocessed_guidelines_px.pdf)
- PX 2023 update (Deutsch et al., NAR): "more than 100 datasets have been reanalysed, assigned RPXD identifiers, and published from jPOSTrepo". — [NAR 2023](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/51/D1/D1539/6825347)
- PX 2026 update: PXD identifiers "are persistent and unique, and are used as the main dataset identifier for all originally submitted datasets"; RPXD assigned to reanalysed datasets; "This first scenario includes those cases when a PX resource directly performs the reanalysis of the datasets (e.g. PeptideAtlas), or when the reanalysis is performed by community members using the infrastructure of a resource (e.g. MassIVE)." — [NAR 2026](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D459/8315797)
- Example RPXD records: RPXD000665 (PeptideAtlas, "old-style reanalysis announcement missing a container"); RPXD055695 (jPOST container). — [RPXD000665](https://proteomecentral.proteomexchange.org/cgi/GetDataset?ID=RPXD000665); [RPXD055695](https://proteomecentral.proteomexchange.org/cgi/GetDataset?ID=RPXD055695)
- Historical PRIDE stance: "no reprocessing of the data is performed because PRIDE aims to reflect the author's analysis view on the experimental data". — [PMC3531176](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3531176)
- Leite et al., Proteomics 2025 (10.1002/pmic.70175), 500 human PX datasets: processed quantitative outputs fully available for 49 %, partially 33.6 %, absent 17.4 %; 189 datasets (37.8 %) supported direct matrix-level reuse; 64 (12.8 %) met all strict criteria — a reusability audit, not a reproduction criterion. — [Wiley](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pmic.70175?af=R)
- Franzetti et al., Proteomes 2026 (10.3390/proteomes14020016) review: defines data reuse as "the systematic reanalysis and integration of publicly available datasets to support precision oncology applications such as biomarker assessment and antibody-drug conjugate target prioritization"; lists harmonisation issues (batch effects, missing values, normalisation) but no acceptance metric. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7382/14/2/16)
- Griss et al. 2016 Nat Methods (10.1038/nmeth.3902) and Martens/Vizcaíno reviews: not retrievable in this session; no criteria statements located.

### Inferences
- The RPXD framework treats a reanalysis as a new, independently valid result with provenance links; it does not require demonstrating agreement with the original study.

- Griss 2016, Vizcaíno/Martens 2017 TIBS statements on "successful reanalysis": not found.

## Key question 6: DIA-NN vs Spectronaut phosphoproteomics agreement (Kitata 2021, Bekker-Jensen 2020, Skowronek 2022, Lou 2023, Pham 2024)

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings
- Kitata et al. 2021 Nat Commun (10.1038/s41467-021-22759-z): hybrid DDA/DIA library "processed with Spectronaut Pulsar at 1% FDR"; "Single-shot DIA was acquired and processed by both library-based DIA (libDIA) and direct DIA (dirDIA) approach by Spectronaut."; "Phosphosite-level quantification was obtained by an in-house customized R program."; class 1 sites at localization probability ≥ 0.75.
