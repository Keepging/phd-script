# Numeric agreement thresholds between two quantitative proteomics analyses: guidelines and reported multi-site / multi-pipeline values

**Methodological caveat (read first).** In this research session every publisher/repository host (nature.com, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, europepmc.org, pubs.acs.org, fda.gov, psidev.info, biorxiv.org, mdpi.com, cancer.gov, etc.) was blocked by the sandbox network policy (HTTP 403 on CONNECT), so no full text could be fetched. All findings below come from web-search result snippets/summaries that surfaced text from the papers. Where a sentence is given in quotation marks it is the wording returned in the snippet; it should be verified against the full text before being quoted in a manuscript. Where a number could not be surfaced it is recorded as a gap, not inferred.

## KQ1. Do HUPO-PSI / MIAPE, CPTAC, FDA-type guidance, MCP/journal guidelines specify a numeric agreement criterion between two analyses?

### Takeaway
No guideline, consortium standard or journal policy was found that defines a numeric pass/fail threshold (direction concordance %, correlation r, or significant-set overlap %) for declaring two quantitative proteomics analyses of the same samples or same raw data "consistent" or "reproducible". MIAPE/MIAPE-Quant are reporting checklists; CPTAC fit-for-purpose criteria address assay precision (CV) for targeted assays, not inter-analysis agreement; the FDA 2018 bioanalytical guidance contains a numeric criterion (incurred-sample reanalysis: >=67% of samples within 20%) but it applies to re-measurement of samples in regulated small-molecule/ligand-binding assays, not to re-processing of proteomics raw data with a second pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- **HUPO-PSI MIAPE-Quant**: described as setting out "the minimum information to report when a mass spectrometry quantitative dataset goes to a database or into journal supplementary material", covering "general features, such as the team and the quantitative method used, and experimental design, such as sample descriptions, replicate numbers, and standards or internal references"; developed alongside mzQuantML; version 1.0. No numeric agreement criterion appears in any surfaced description. — [MIBBI MIAPE page](https://mibbi.sourceforge.net/projects/MIAPE.shtml); [Martínez-Bartolomé et al., "Guidelines for reporting quantitative mass spectrometry based experiments in proteomics" (J Proteomics 2013), record](https://portalrecerca.uab.cat/ca/publications/guidelines-for-reporting-quantitative-mass-spectrometry-based-exp/); [RRID SCR_003158](https://rrid.site/resolver/SCR_003158). Numeric threshold: **not found**.
- **MIAPE parent framework**: "MIAPE is a parent document with modules for specific technologies" — a reporting-content framework, not an acceptance-criteria framework. — [MIBBI](https://mibbi.sourceforge.net/projects/MIAPE.shtml). Numeric threshold: **not found**.
- **CPTAC Assay Portal guidance**: "aligning with the research community's 'fit-for-purpose' validation requirements of targeted proteomics assays"; "Characterization consists of a set of five experiments. Experiments 1 and 2 are required for upload of assays into the portal"; assays are "mostly 'Tier 2' assays". These are per-assay characterization experiments (response curve, repeatability, etc.) for targeted (MRM/PRM) assays; no criterion for agreement between two analyses of the same data was surfaced. — [NCI DCTD Assay Portal guidance](https://dctd.cancer.gov/drug-discovery-development/assays/proteomic-assay-portal/guidance); [Assay characterization guidance documents](https://proteomics.cancer.gov/assay-portal/about/assay-characterization-guidance-documents). Numeric inter-analysis threshold: **not found**.
- **Carr et al. 2014, MCP 13:907–917 (DOI 10.1074/mcp.M113.036095), "Targeted peptide measurements in biology and medicine: best practices ... fit-for-purpose approach"**: the authors "observed that a wide range of criteria was being used to declare an assay successfully developed, with no consensus on which criteria are acceptable" and proposed "three tiers of assays distinguished by their performance and extent of analytical characterization". The tier-specific CV cut-offs could not be surfaced in this session (see Gaps). The paper concerns targeted assay validation, not agreement between two discovery pipelines. — [Carr 2014 record (PMC3945918)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3945918); [Thermo blog summary of the 3-tier system](https://thermofisher.com/blog/proteomics/new-3-tier-system-for-targeted-mass-spectrometry-assays).
- **MCP / JBC journal guidelines**: the JBC proteomics checklist "points authors to the MCP guidelines and asks them to state the number of technical and biological replicates", "methods to determine significance, power analysis or test(s) applied", software name and version. No fold-change concordance or inter-pipeline agreement criterion surfaced. — [JBC proteomics checklist](https://legacyfileshare.elsevier.com/promis_misc/jbc-proteomics-checklist.pdf). Numeric threshold: **not found**.
- **FDA Bioanalytical Method Validation Guidance for Industry (May 2018)**: official PDF at [fda.gov/media/70858/download](https://www.fda.gov/media/70858/download) (mirror: [HHS copy](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/sites/default/files/hhs-guidance-documents/FDA/Bioanalytical-Method-Validation-Guidance-for-Industry.pdf)). Scope: "validating bioanalytical methods used in nonclinical and human clinical pharmacology, bioavailability, and bioequivalence studies". The incurred-sample-reanalysis (ISR) criterion, as described in a secondary AAPS J source: "at least 67% of ISR samples that have reanalyzed concentrations within 20% of their original concentrations when normalized to their means" (small molecules); traced to the 2008 ISR Workshop and adopted by EMA, Health Canada and FDA. — [AAPS J ISR paper (PMC4287300)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4287300); [Springer 2019 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1208/s12248-019-0293-2). Cross-validation language (from a 2013 draft summary, secondary): "Cross-validation with spiked matrix standards and subject samples should be conducted at each site or laboratory to establish inter-laboratory reliability, and should also be considered when data from different analytical techniques, such as LCMS/MS vs. ELISA, from different studies are included in a regulatory submission." — [BioPharma-Reporter summary of the 2013 draft](https://www.biopharma-reporter.com/Article/2013/09/18/FDA-Draft-guidance-on-bioanalytical-validation-reflects-tech-advances). The guidance concerns re-measurement of samples by a validated single-analyte assay; **it does not address agreement between two data-processing pipelines applied to the same proteomics raw data** (no such language surfaced).
- **Proteomics QC literature**: the "framework for quality control in quantitative proteomics" (2024) describes "adaptable quality control (QC) measures for sample preparation, system function, and quantitative analysis, rather than fixed cutoffs"; a statistical-process-control paper states "thresholds are experiment- and instrument-specific and are determined empirically from user-defined quality control standards". — [bioRxiv 2024.04.12.589318](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.04.12.589318.full.pdf) / [PMC11030400](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11030400/); [SPC for LC-MS/MS proteomics](https://www.academia.edu/18955676/Implementation_of_Statistical_Process_Control_for_Proteomic_Experiments_Via_LC_MS_MS). Numeric inter-analysis threshold: **not found**.
- **Bittremieux et al. 2018, Mass Spectrom Rev 37(5):697–711 (DOI 10.1002/mas.21544)**: abstract (per repository record) covers "potential sources of variability across sample preparation, liquid chromatography, mass spectrometry, and bioinformatics, along with how to monitor and remedy them" and QC samples "to assess instrument performance". No numeric acceptance criterion for agreement between runs or pipelines was surfaced. — [UHasselt record](https://documentserver.uhasselt.be/handle/1942/24162); companion 2017 Proteomics review "Computational quality control tools for mass spectrometry proteomics" [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/835508). Numeric threshold: **not found**.
- **ProteoBench** (community DIA/DDA quantification benchmark): modules compute per-precursor signal sums, ratio metrics etc.; "Users can inspect results privately or submit a benchmark run ... visible to everyone". No pass/fail threshold is described. — [ProteoBench docs](https://proteobench.readthedocs.io/en/latest/available-modules/active-modules/5-quant-lfq-ion-dia-diapasef/). Numeric threshold: **not found**.

### Inferences
- The field's formal documents regulate *what to report* (MIAPE), *how to validate an assay* (CPTAC tiers, FDA BMV) and *how to monitor instruments* (QC metrics). None converts to a criterion for "two analyses of the same data agree". Any threshold used in a DIA reanalysis manuscript would therefore have to be justified empirically, e.g., by reference to the ranges reported in KQ2–KQ4 below, and explicitly labelled as study-specific.
- The FDA ISR "67% within 20%" rule is the only widely cited numeric *re-analysis* acceptance criterion in analytical chemistry, but it applies to repeat measurement of biological samples with a validated assay, not to re-processing raw MS data; citing it as an analogue requires that caveat.

### Gaps
- Exact wording of FDA 2018 sections on ISR and cross-validation could not be fetched (fda.gov blocked); the 67%/20% wording above is from a secondary AAPS source.
- Carr 2014 tier-specific CV limits not retrievable; verify from PMC3945918 before citing.
- HUPO-PSI psidev.info pages not reachable; MIAPE-Quant itemized checklist not inspected.
- Current MCP "Mass Spec Guidelines" page not retrieved.

## KQ2. Collins et al. 2017 Nat Commun (DOI 10.1038/s41467-017-00249-5): inter-laboratory SWATH-MS reproducibility values

### Takeaway
Eleven laboratories; 4,077 proteins detected in >80% of 229 files; protein-level CVs after peptide-level median normalization were ~8.3% intra-day, ~11.9% inter-day and ~22.0% inter-site (medians). No pass/fail criterion is stated; inter-site Pearson correlation values were not surfaced.

### Cited Findings
- "Using SWATH-mass spectrometry data acquisition, the authors detected and quantified more than 4,000 proteins consistently from HEK293 cells"; 11 laboratories; "229 SWATH-MS files from the 11 sites worldwide"; 30 SIS peptides diluted into 1 µg HEK293 digest. — [Nature Communications 8:291](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-00249-5); [bioRxiv preprint 074567](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/074567v1.full)
- "a total of 4077 proteins were detected in >80% of all samples"; "the CVs after peptide-level median normalization were 8.3 ± 16.2, 11.9 ± 17.2, and 22.0 ± 17.4% at the intra-day, inter-day, and inter-site levels, respectively" (snippet of published text). — [Nature Communications 8:291](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-00249-5)
- Press summary: "the overall median CV across the 11 sites (229 data files) and ~4000 proteins was ~22%". — [SCIEX blog](https://sciex.com/community/blogs/blogs/multi-laboratory-study-highlights-the-quantitative-reproducibility-of-swath-acquisition-nature-communications-paper); [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/analysis/articles/swath-ms-generates-highly-reproducible-data-293812)
- Published version: "median number of proteins detected per file is 4548 from a median of 31,886 peak groups" (preprint gave 4,710 / 34,480). — [Nature Communications 8:291](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-00249-5)
- Fragment (context uncertain): "SWATH-MS demonstrated improved intra-lab reproducibility compared with MS1 with CV values of 8.8 and 13.2%, respectively". — [ResearchGate full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319175635_Multi-laboratory_assessment_of_reproducibility_qualitative_and_quantitative_performance_of_SWATH-mass_spectrometry)
- Motivation statement: "while previous SWATH-mass spectrometry studies have shown high intra-lab reproducibility, this has not been evaluated between labs". — [Nature Communications 8:291](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-00249-5)

### Inferences
- The study reports *descriptive* dispersion (CV) and does not declare a threshold; the inter-site median CV of ~22% is the de-facto benchmark figure from this paper most often quoted.

### Gaps
- Inter-laboratory Pearson/Spearman correlation coefficients (the paper has a correlation matrix) were not surfaced; concluding sentences of Abstract/Discussion could not be quoted verbatim.

## KQ3. Navarro et al. 2016 Nat Biotechnol (DOI 10.1038/nbt.3685): agreement between SWATH software tools

### Takeaway
Five tools (OpenSWATH, SWATH 2.0, Skyline, Spectronaut, DIA-Umpire) were benchmarked on hybrid-proteome samples with known expected log2 ratios (0 human, +3.3 E. coli, −3.3 yeast) using LFQbench; the stated outcome is that "after optimization all tools gave highly convergent identification and reliable quantification". No numeric pass/fail criterion for tool agreement was defined; per-tool accuracy/precision numbers were not surfaced.

### Cited Findings
- Tools: "OpenSWATH, SWATH 2.0, Skyline, Spectronaut and DIA-Umpire"; samples: "Two proteome-hybrid samples (A and B) contained known quantities of peptides from human, yeast, and E. coli", run "in triplicate on two TripleTOF instruments (5600 and 6600) with two SWATH window setups (32 fixed and 64 variable windows), yielding four benchmarking datasets". — [PubMed 27701404](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27701404/); [Nature Biotechnology article](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt.3685)
- "The expected log2 fold change (B vs. A) is 0 for human proteins, 3.3 for E. coli proteins and −3.3 for yeast proteins." — [DIA-NN paper describing the LFQbench design (PMC6949130)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6949130/)
- "The authors wrote LFQbench, an R package, to calculate precision and accuracy metrics and to report identification performance, robustness and specificity." Outcome: "LFQbench helped developers find problems in their algorithms and improve them, and after optimization all tools gave highly convergent identification and reliable quantification." — [EMBL-EBI news](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/about/news/service-news/benchmarking-data-analysis-software-LFQbench); [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27701404/)
- Data: ProteomeXchange PXD002952; code IFIproteomics/LFQbench. — [PXD002952](https://proteomecentral.proteomexchange.org/cgi/GetDataset?ID=PXD002952)

### Inferences
- The LFQbench paradigm judges each tool against *ground truth* (spike-in ratios), not tool-vs-tool agreement; "convergent" is qualitative. It is therefore not a source for an inter-pipeline agreement threshold.

### Gaps
- Per-tool accuracy (median deviation from expected log2 ratio) and precision (SD of log ratios) values, and the Discussion's closing sentences, were not retrievable.

## KQ4. CPTAC inter-laboratory studies (Tabb 2010; Paulovich 2010; Addona 2009): agreement values and any criteria

### Takeaway
Tabb et al. 2010 report peptide-ID overlap between technical replicate pairs of 35–60% (medians ~44–53%); Addona 2009 report MRM assays "highly reproducible within and across laboratories"; none of the CPTAC papers surfaced defines a numeric acceptance threshold for agreement between analyses.

### Cited Findings
- Tabb et al. 2010, J Proteome Res 9(2):761–776 (DOI 10.1021/pr9006365; PMC2818771): "Peptide lists from pairs of technical replicates overlapped by 35−60%"; NCI-20 mixture "median 44% overlap in the peptides identified between pairs of replicates"; Sigma UPS1 "46% overlap for peptides"; yeast "45 percent"; "The median peptide repeatability, however, was essentially identical between the low and high loads, both with median values of 53%"; data from "144 LC-MS/MS experiments on four Thermo LTQ and four Orbitrap instruments". — [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Repeatability-and-reproducibility-in-proteomic-by-Tabb-Vega-Montoto/832fc890098e735c1d0b1d942c19458bcb0c4680); [MSU-hosted PDF](https://rtsf.natsci.msu.edu/_assets/files/proteomics/papers/Repeatability-Reproducibility_in_proteomicIDs_by_LCMS_nihms165866.pdf); [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/pr9006365)
- "Orbitraps achieved 9 percent to 18 percent greater peptide repeatability than LTQs for the yeast samples." — [GenomeWeb coverage](https://www.genomeweb.com/archive/nci-cptac-study-provides-insight-variability-lc-ms-systems)
- Acceptance criterion in Tabb 2010: **not found** in surfaced text (the paper is descriptive).
- Paulovich et al. 2010, MCP 9(2):242–254 (DOI 10.1074/mcp.M900222-MCP200; PMC2830837): yeast performance standard "to benchmark detection of differentially expressed proteins at different concentrations in a complex matrix"; distributed via NIST. Numeric reproducibility/CV values: **not surfaced**. — [Synapse MSKCC record](https://synapse.mskcc.org/synapse/works/4832)
- Addona et al. 2009, Nat Biotechnol 27(7):633–641 (DOI 10.1038/nbt.1546): "these assays were highly reproducible within and across laboratories and instrument platforms, with sensitivity down to low µg/mL protein levels in unfractionated plasma"; reanalysed by Xia, Sedransk & Feng 2011 (PLOS ONE) who note Addona "reported coefficients of variation for linear response, percent recovery, LoD, and LoQ". Specific CV values **not surfaced**. — [PMC3027641 (variance component reanalysis)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3027641/)
- Later multi-site plasma study (Kardell et al. 2025, J Proteome Res 24:1017–1029, DOI 10.1021/acs.jproteome.4c00644; PMC11894660): eight MSCoreSys labs; "Protein IDs ran between 300 and 400 across instruments"; "71 were reproducibly detectable across all setups in both serum and plasma. Twenty-two of these are FDA-approved biomarkers that were reproducibly quantified (CV < 20% with label-free quantification)"; pairwise interlaboratory ID overlap "greater than 60% in most cases". — [PMC11894660](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11894660/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1535390725000666)
- Quartet protein reference materials (Tian et al. 2023, Genome Biol, DOI 10.1186/s13059-023-03048-y): "15 laboratories with nine conventional LC–MS/MS platforms"; "relative abundance of over 12,000 proteins on 816 mass spectrometry files"; "low reproducibility across large-scale experiments remains one of the most critical challenges in proteomics". Inter-lab correlation values **not surfaced**. — [Genome Biology](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13059-023-03048-y)

### Inferences
- CPTAC-era benchmarks quantify *identification* overlap (≈45–53% per replicate pair in 2010 DDA; >60% between labs in 2025 plasma DIA) and precision (CV < 20% as an informal "reproducibly quantified" label), not fold-change direction or significant-set overlap between pipelines.
- The "CV < 20%" phrase in Kardell 2025 is used as a working label, not as a consortium-wide criterion.

### Gaps
- Addona 2009 / Paulovich 2010 numeric CVs; Quartet inter-lab correlations; Tabb 2010 protein-level overlap figures.

## KQ5. Transcriptomics analogues (MAQC-I 2006; SEQC/MAQC-III 2014; MAQC-II 2010): metrics and values — NOT proteomics

### Takeaway
MAQC-I used "percentage of overlapping genes" (POG) between fold-change-ranked DEG lists and reported inter-site/inter-platform overlaps generally ≥60%, with many pairings ≥80% between platforms and ~90% within platform; SEQC reported that fold-change + expression filtering produced "good inter-site agreement" of DEG lists and gave cross-platform log2-FC correlations, but neither consortium set a pass/fail threshold. MAQC-II concerned predictive-model validation, not cross-platform concordance. These are microarray/RNA-seq results and are explicitly **not proteomics**.

### Cited Findings
- MAQC-I, Shi et al. 2006, Nat Biotechnol 24(9):1151–1161 (DOI 10.1038/nbt1239): abstract — "Expression data on four titration pools from two distinct reference RNA samples were generated at multiple test sites"; "We show intraplatform consistency across test sites as well as a high level of interplatform concordance in terms of genes identified as differentially expressed." — [PubMed 16964229](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16964229/); [Nature Biotechnology](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt1239)
- MAQC-I result summary (from a hosted copy of the main paper): "for all but the NCI test sites, the gene list overlap is at least 60% for each test site comparison (both directions), with many site pairings achieving 80% or more between platforms and 90% within platforms"; a fragment mentions "an average of about 89% overlap" (comparison not determinable from snippet). — [Biocat-hosted PDF of Shi 2006](https://www.biocat.com/bc/pdf/MAQC_MS-3_MainPaper_Shi.pdf); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6826866_The_MicroArray_Quality_Control_MAQC_Project_Shows_Inter-_and_Intraplatform_Reproducibility_of_Gene_Expression_Measurements)
- Detection-call concordance "used the 12,091 common genes". — [Biocat PDF](https://www.biocat.com/bc/pdf/MAQC_MS-3_MainPaper_Shi.pdf)
- POG definition: "the number of genes in common divided by the number of genes in each of the two equal-length lists"; MAQC "recommended fold change ranking using a nonstringent P-value cutoff for gene selection". — [Reproducibility of DEG lists (Nature Precedings 2007)](https://www.nature.com/articles/npre.2007.306.1.pdf); [BMC Bioinformatics 8:412](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2105-8-412)
- Illustrative follow-up values: "with P-value ranking, the top 100 genes from two platforms shared only 14 genes (POG = 14%). Fold-change ranking shared 64 genes"; another reanalysis: "average POG of 13% and 51% (based on the gene selection method of fold-change ranking) before and after data filtering". — [BMC Bioinformatics 9(S9):S10](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2105-9-S9-S10); [Algorithms Mol Biol 4:7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1748-7188-4-7)
- Directional overlap used in MAQC follow-ups: "The percentage of overlapping genes with the same directional change in expression was 89.94% (7,690 out of 8,550 common genes) when no p-value cutoff was applied to either experiment" (expired vs unexpired Affymetrix arrays, MAQC RNA). — [BMC Bioinformatics 11(S6):S10 / PMC3026357](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3026357/)
- SEQC/MAQC-III 2014, Nat Biotechnol 32:903–914 (DOI 10.1038/nbt.2957; PMC4321899): "removing small fold-changes, along with excluding low-expression measurements, reduced the false discovery rate considerably. These filters also achieved good inter-site agreement of lists of differentially expressed genes"; "Microarrays showed higher inter-site reproducibility when only the differentially expressed genes with the strongest fold change were considered"; Figure 6 "reports Pearson and Spearman coefficients for log2 fold-change estimates across platforms on 843 selected genes, with good and similar concordances observed between relative expression measures from the HiSeq 2000 and SOLiD platforms". A follow-up states SEQC found "dependable differential expression was feasible for less than two-thirds of the genome". — [PMC4321899](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4321899/); [Nature Biotechnology](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt.2957); [Sci Rep 2020 pipeline-variability paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-59516-z)
- SEQC-derived POG convention: a later study "counts a gene list as agreeing only when the direction of fold change matches; genes with opposite directions are excluded from the intersection". — [Biology Direct 11 (2016)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13062-016-0169-7)
- MAQC-II (Shi et al. 2010, Nat Biotechnol 28:827): "the MAQC-II study on developing and validating microarray-based predictive models" — not a cross-platform concordance study. — [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/the-microarray-quality-control-maqc-ii-study-of-common-practices-/)
- Non-proteomics direction-concordance range (cancer transcriptomics, 2026 arXiv): "genome-wide direction concordance ranged from 57.6% to 78.4%. Restricting to genes significant in both cohorts raised it to 85.6% to 98.7%." — [arXiv 2608.02796](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02796)
- Caution on FC cut-offs: Vacc-SeqQC found "applying fold-change cutoffs for DEG detection reduced inter-set agreement and should be used with caution, if at all". — [Front Immunol 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2022.1093242/full)

### Inferences
- MAQC/SEQC produced *descriptive* concordance ranges (POG ≥60–90%) and a *procedural* recommendation (FC ranking + lenient p) rather than a pass/fail rule; a proteomics manuscript citing them should say so and flag the modality difference.

### Gaps
- Exact MAQC-I per-platform POG tables and SEQC Figure 3/6 numeric coefficients not retrievable; "~89%" fragment context unresolved.

## KQ6. Proteomics papers reporting sign/direction concordance, fold-change correlation, or significant-set overlap between two pipelines or two analyses of the same data

### Takeaway
No proteomics paper surfaced that uses a pre-declared sign-concordance threshold. Reported inter-pipeline/reanalysis agreement values are heterogeneous: fold-change correlation between original and reanalysed DIA results R = 0.52–0.84 (Walzer 2022); protein-intensity correlations between DIA tools r ≈ 0.85–0.89 (TIMS-PASEF workflows) up to median 0.997–0.999 (Lou 2023, replicate-level); significant-set overlaps range from ~38% (TraianProt vs Proteome Discoverer) to 1,130 shared DEPs out of 1,516 (Spectronaut) / 1,993 (DIA-NN).

### Cited Findings
- Walzer et al. 2022, Sci Data 9:335 (DOI 10.1038/s41597-022-01380-9; PMC9197839), reanalysis of 10 PRIDE DIA datasets (1,278 SWATH runs) vs original publications: "Overlap of quantified protein fold-changes between original and reanalysed results ranged widely across contrasts, from about 2,992 of 4,572 down to 863 of 1,868. Correlation ranged from R = 0.84 to 0.52. Applying significance and fold-change cutoffs reduced the overlap further, and one contrast had almost no overlap among significant proteins." Author wording: "a substantial overlap of quantified protein expression values between the originally reported results and the reanalysed ones, and an increased number of protein signals of lower intensity, that differed between the analyses to a greater extent, being picked up as the number of quantified proteins increase". — [Scientific Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01380-9); [PMC9197839](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9197839/)
- Lou et al. 2023, Nat Commun 14:94 ("Benchmarking commonly used software suites and analysis workflows for DIA proteomics and phosphoproteomics"): "DIA-NN had the highest protein-quantification correlation in the comparisons, with median coefficients of about 0.997 to 0.999"; "Spectronaut and DIA-NN both detected similar numbers of differentially expressed proteins for a 2:1 expected ratio". — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35740-1)
- TIMS-PASEF workflow comparison (FragPipe/IonQuant, DIA-NN, Spectronaut; bioRxiv 2021.11.29.470373): "Pearson correlations between workflows were higher between DIA-NN and Spectronaut workflows (0.85 to 0.89) than the cross-tool pairs involving other software, while the lowest correlation was between IonQuant and Spectronaut." — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.11.29.470373v1.full)
- Spectronaut vs DIA-NN on LUAD biopsies (ACS Omega, 2025/26 issue): ">7,600 proteins, with 7,180 proteins common"; Spectronaut "1,250 upregulated and 266 downregulated", DIA-NN "1,819 and 174"; "1,130 differentially expressed proteins were common to both platforms"; "Of the top 50 differentially expressed proteins, 34 were shared". — [ACS Omega](https://pubs.acs.org/acsodf/article/11/5/8080/5078047/Spectronaut-and-DIA-NN-A-Comparison-of-their)
- TraianProt vs Proteome Discoverer (arXiv 2412.15806): "135 out of 353 proteins (38%) were identified as differentially expressed by both, and for the subset of proteins consistently identified as significant by both approaches, fold changes and associated p-values were highly concordant". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.15806)
- Reanalysis discrepancy example (reporting-shortcomings study): "large discrepancies in the analysis results (e.g., 13,068 vs. 4,923 proteins; 108 vs. 11 differentially expressed proteins)" attributed to missing decoy details, proprietary outputs and vague normalization/statistics parameters. — [PMC7614296](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7614296)
- Fröhlich et al. 2022, Nat Commun 13:2622: "1428 distinct data analysis workflows, judged by how well each one identified differentially abundant proteins"; findings on libraries/tests; inter-software overlap values **not surfaced**. — [PMC9098472](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9098472)
- Package disagreement statement: results of LFQ differential-expression packages on three public datasets "can vary significantly" between packages and parameter settings. — [Nat Commun 2024 ensemble-inference paper (PMC11082229)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11082229/)
- Jaccard usage in proteomics (FedProt): Jaccard of significant-protein lists vs centralized reference "0.99 and another 0.81". — [arXiv 2407.15220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.15220)
- Direction-based proteomics integration (2026 bioRxiv): scores "concordance of up- and down-regulated proteins with odds ratios rather than a simple percentage" across quantMS and CPTAC datasets. — [bioRxiv 2026.04.19.718092](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.19.718092v1.full)
- Cross-platform (Olink vs MS) plasma comparison defines "directional agreement as the share of proteins that change in the same direction on both platforms" and reports "strong concordance in estimated differences even though only a modest overlap was significant" (percentage not in snippet). — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397265117_Comparative_evaluation_of_Olink_Explore_3072_and_mass_spectrometry_with_peptide_fractionation_for_plasma_proteomics)
- quantms (Nat Methods 2024) reanalysed 83 ProteomeXchange datasets; "quantified more proteins than MaxQuant at similar accuracy, though it underestimated true fold changes at low concentrations". — [Nature Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02343-1)

### Inferences
- Reported agreement between two pipelines/analyses of the same samples spans r ≈ 0.5–0.9 for fold changes at the dataset level and ≈0.85–0.999 for protein intensities, and significant-set overlaps of roughly 40–75%; the lower bounds come from reanalyses with different libraries/inference, the upper bounds from replicate-level intensity correlations. These are *observed* ranges, not criteria.
- No proteomics study surfaced reports a "% same direction" statistic with a threshold; the only threshold-like directional figures found are transcriptomic (57.6–78.4% genome-wide; 85.6–98.7% among jointly significant genes).
