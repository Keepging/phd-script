## Key question 1 — Fröhlich et al. 2022, Nat Commun 13:2622, DOI 10.1038/s41467-022-30094-0 ("Benchmarking of analysis strategies for data-independent acquisition proteomics using a large-scale dataset comprising inter-patient heterogeneity")

### Takeaway
The paper compares four DIA software tools (DIA-NN, Skyline, OpenSWATH, Spectronaut) combined with several spectral libraries, sparsity-reduction, normalisation and statistical-test options (1428 workflows) on an E. coli-spike-in/human lymph-node dataset, and scores each workflow by its ability to recover the truly differentially abundant (spike-in) proteins (sensitivity / false positives against a ground truth). No pairwise tool-vs-tool agreement metric (fold-change correlation, sign concordance, Jaccard of significant sets) was found in the retrievable text.

### Cited Findings
- Pipelines compared: "four DIA software tools (DIA-NN, Skyline, OpenSwath and Spectronaut)" (excerpt, from a review describing the paper) — [ScienceDirect review S1535947624000902](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1535947624000902); the paper itself: [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30094-0)
- Workflow count: "Combining spectral libraries, DIA software, sparsity reduction, normalization, and statistical tests results in 1428 distinct data analysis workflows" (excerpt, Results) — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30094-0)
- Design: "spiked E. coli peptides in known ratios into peptide preparations of lymph nodes of 92 individuals and analyzed the data with different spectral libraries and DIA software suites"; "The dataset comprises 118 LC-MS/MS runs with more than 12 million MS2 spectra" (excerpts) — [PMC9098472](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9098472/); [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30094-0)
- Identification threshold used: "We first assessed the number of identified and quantified proteins after a 1% protein FDR cutoff had been applied for each workflow." (excerpt, Results) — [PMC9098472](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9098472/)
- Agreement/performance metric actually used: workflows are "evaluated on their ability to correctly identify differentially abundant proteins" using bootstrap datasets of varying sample size ("the whole range of bootstrap datasets") (excerpts) — [WU Vienna record](https://research.wu.ac.at/en/publications/benchmarking-of-analysis-strategies-for-data-independent-acquisit/); [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30094-0)
- Two reference lists define the ground truth: an "Intersection" list and a "Combined" list; "The 'overall sensitivity' performance is assessed by including proteins of all workflows into the reference protein list." (excerpt) — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30094-0)
- Conclusion sentence (abstract, excerpt): "a gas phase fractionation refined spectral library in combination with DIA-NN and Significance Analysis of Microarrays performed reliably" — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30094-0)
- Conclusion sentence (Results, excerpt): "Spectronaut's 'DirectDIA' performs best, while DIA-NN, Skyline and Spectronaut all perform well using the more classical DDA spectral libraries generated by MaxQuant and MSFragger." — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30094-0)
- Conclusion sentence (Results, excerpt): "When all proteins were included appearing in at least one DIA software-library combination, DIA-NN in combination with the in silico predicted DIA-NN spectral library performed best. If only those proteins are taken into account, which were found in the dataset of the respective DIA software-library combination, Spectronaut's 'DirectDIA' excelled." — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30094-0)
- Conclusion sentence (excerpt): "All DIA software suites benefit from using a gas-phase fractionated spectral library, irrespective of the library refinement used." — [PMC9098472](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9098472/)
- Conclusion sentence on false positives (excerpt; shown for the preprint version): "Furthermore, DIA-NN and Spectronaut robustly avoided the false detection of truly absent proteins." — [ResearchGate preprint record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354720949_In-Depth_Benchmarking_of_DIA-type_Proteomics_Data_Analysis_Strategies_Using_a_Large-Scale_Benchmark_Dataset_Comprising_Inter-Patient_Heterogeneity)
- Paraphrase that appeared in a search summary (wording NOT confirmed): "On average, DIA-NN performs slightly better than the other software solutions when using [the Intersection] list." — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30094-0)
- Third-party summary (review, excerpt): "The choice of DIA analysis software and the spectral library it uses has the most significant impact on the accuracy of identifying differentially abundant proteins."; "Using gas-phase fractionated (GPF) spectral libraries generally improved performance" — [Lacuna summary page](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/benchmarking-of-analysis-strategies-for-data-independent-acquisition-proteomics/wrk_23b968f2ee29e0dc5bca94ce0e92e15f)
- Third-party summary (2024 clinical-DIA review, excerpt): "using Spectronaut and DIA-NN led to fewer false identifications than OpenSWATH and Skyline" — [ScienceDirect review S1535947624000902](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1535947624000902)
- Third-party summary (same 2024 review family, excerpt; attributed to "a separate study", attribution to Fröhlich NOT confirmed): "Skyline and OpenSWATH gave the most accurate fold changes, while DIA-NN and Spectronaut overestimated them" — [PMC11380018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11380018/)

### Inferences
- The paper's agreement framework is "each pipeline vs. a spike-in ground truth" (sensitivity/false positives of differentially abundant E. coli proteins), not "pipeline A vs. pipeline B" on the same protein list. Any pairwise tool-tool metric would have to be computed by the reader from its released workflow outputs.
- The only thresholds confirmed from retrieved text are identification-level (1% protein FDR). A statistical threshold for calling differential abundance (adjusted p, pAUC cut-off) must exist in the Methods but was not retrievable.

### Gaps
- Pearson/Spearman correlation of log2 fold changes or intensities between DIA-NN and Spectronaut (or any other pair): not found.
- Sign/direction concordance of differentially abundant proteins between tools: not found.
- Overlap (Jaccard / %) of significant protein sets between tools: not found (only the "Intersection"/"Combined" reference-list concept was retrieved, without numbers).
- Exact TP/FP counts, pAUC values, CV values per software: not found.
- The adjusted-p / FDR cut-off used for differential abundance, and whether any value functions as a pass/fail threshold other than the 1% protein FDR: not found.
- Discussion-section conclusion sentences: not retrievable.

## Key question 2 — Gotti et al. 2021, J Proteome Res 20(10):4801–4814, DOI 10.1021/acs.jproteome.1c00490 ("Extensive and Accurate Benchmarking of DIA Acquisition Methods and Software Tools Using a Complex Proteomic Standard"); preprint bioRxiv DOI 10.1101/2020.11.03.365585; companion data paper Data in Brief 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.dib.2022.107829

### Takeaway
Six tools (DIA-NN, DIA-Umpire, OpenSWATH, ScaffoldDIA, Skyline, Spectronaut) × 4 DIA window schemes (36 workflows) were scored on a UPS1-in-E. coli dilution series by identification counts, quantification linearity and reproducibility, and sensitivity/specificity of differential detection over 28 pairwise concentration comparisons; all outputs filtered at 1% FDR / q < 0.01. No tool-vs-tool correlation or concordance values were retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- Design and tools (excerpt, abstract/preprint): "Using a standard human 48 proteins mixture (UPS1 – Sigma) at 8 different concentrations in an E. coli proteome background, they tested 36 workflows including 4 different DIA window acquisition schemes and 6 different software tools (DIA-NN, DIA-Umpire, OpenSWATH, ScaffoldDIA, Skyline and Spectronaut)" — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.11.03.365585.full.pdf); [ACS JPR](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jproteome.1c00490)
- Data scale and filtering (companion data article, excerpt): "96 DIA raw files covering eight concentrations, each analyzed in triplicate"; "Outputs were validated at 1% FDR or q-value below 0.01"; raw data PXD026600 — [PMC8841991](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8841991/); [ScienceDirect Data in Brief](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352340922000415)
- Metrics used (excerpt): ranking "based on the number of proteins identified, quantification linearity and reproducibility, as well as sensitivity and specificity"; "across 28 pairwise comparisons" — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354336993_Extensive_and_Accurate_Benchmarking_of_DIA_Acquisition_Methods_and_Software_Tools_Using_a_Complex_Proteomic_Standard)
- Caveat sentence (preprint, excerpt): "it was not possible to draw any conclusions about software performance in relation to protein abundance in the sample" — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.11.03.365585.full.pdf)
- Third-party characterisation (review, excerpt): "DIA-NN consistently identified most peptides and proteins of the E. coli background in FASTA mode, while Spectronaut identified the most peptides in library mode" — [MCP survey 2023](https://www.mcponline.org/article/S1535-9476(24)00002-1/fulltext)
- Third-party characterisation (review, excerpt): "DIA-NN could be considered the most robust tool for library-free DIA analyses", and "Spectronaut and ScaffoldDIA are able to successfully fulfil these tasks" — [ScienceDirect review S1535947624000902](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1535947624000902)
- Third-party characterisation (citing paper abstract, excerpt): Gotti et al. found that "DIA-NN reached the highest sensitivity while maintaining a good compromise on the reproducibility and accuracy levels" — [bioRxiv 2023.06.02.543441](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.06.02.543441v1.full)
- Version caveat from a 2024 comparison (excerpt): "Gotti et al adopted the 2020/04/20 version of DIA-NN" and "there have been significant improvements in the performance between these two versions" — [MCP survey 2023](https://www.mcponline.org/article/S1535-9476(24)00002-1/fulltext)
- Design caveat from a review (excerpt): the authors did not include a "zero" spike-in condition — [MCP survey 2023](https://www.mcponline.org/article/S1535-9476(24)00002-1/fulltext)
- A reprocessed version of the dataset is on Zenodo ("DIA benchmarking dataset (based on Gotti et al., 2021)") — [Zenodo 8063173](https://zenodo.org/records/8063173)

### Inferences
- As in Fröhlich, agreement is measured against a known ground truth (expected UPS1 ratios), via linearity, reproducibility and sensitivity/specificity, rather than by pairwise tool correlation.
- The only threshold confirmed is identification-level (1% FDR / q < 0.01).

### Gaps
- Between-tool fold-change correlation, sign concordance, overlap/Jaccard of significant UPS1 sets: not found.
- Exact linearity (R²), CV, sensitivity/specificity or ROC/AUC values per tool: not found.
- Verbatim conclusion sentences of the published JPR abstract/Discussion: not retrievable (ACS blocked).
- Fold-change or p-value cut-offs used to declare a UPS1 protein "detected as differential" in the 28 comparisons: not found.

## Key question 3 — Lou et al. 2023, Nat Commun 14:94, DOI 10.1038/s41467-022-35740-1 ("Benchmarking commonly used software suites and analysis workflows for DIA proteomics and phosphoproteomics")

### Takeaway
DIA-NN, Spectronaut, MaxDIA and Skyline (×7 spectral libraries) were evaluated on mouse-brain-membrane-in-yeast spike-in data (expected ratios 1:4 to 2:1) acquired on Orbitrap HF-X and timsTOF, plus phosphopeptide standards and a TNF-α phosphoproteome; metrics retrievable were identification coverage, FDR control, missing-value rate, quantification variation and numbers of regulated sites. Inter-tool fold-change correlation and overlap of significant proteins were explicitly requested but not found in any retrievable excerpt.

### Cited Findings
- Pipelines and libraries (abstract, excerpt): "We evaluate four commonly used software suites (DIA-NN, Spectronaut, MaxDIA and Skyline) combined with seven different spectral libraries in global proteome analysis." — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35740-1)
- Design (excerpt): "DIA benchmark data sets simulating the regulation of thousands of proteins in a complex background ... collected on both an Orbitrap and a timsTOF instruments"; mouse brain membrane proteins spiked into a yeast background, "the other six samples yielding expected mouse membrane protein ratios from 1:4 to 2:1 relative to a reference" — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35740-1); [PMC9822986](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9822986/)
- Library design (excerpt): "The library generated by FragPipe serves as the universal library used by four software suites"; software-specific DDA libraries from MaxDIA, Skyline and Spectronaut; "three DDA-independent libraries (two whole-proteome in silico libraries and one directDIA library) were generated and processed by DIA-NN, MaxDIA, and Spectronaut" — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35740-1)
- Coverage result (excerpt): with the universal library "DIA-NN, Skyline and Spectronaut gave similar protein coverage, but Skyline's FDR control was insufficient"; "With a software-specific DDA-dependent library, Spectronaut attained the highest coverage" — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35740-1)
- FDR-control result with numbers (excerpt): DIA-NN and Spectronaut "exerted equally adequate FDR control (<1.5% and 0.32% false ID on protein and precursor levels)", with DIA-NN "modestly ahead" — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35740-1)
- Data completeness (excerpt): Skyline "gave the lowest data completeness, with 36.9–40.7% missing values for two data sets" — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35740-1)
- Phosphoproteome, Fig. 5 (legend/results excerpts): MCF-7 cells stimulated with TNF-α ± IKK inhibitor TPCA-1; "Panel b shows the number of TNF-α-regulated phosphosites from the HF-X data and the TIMS data with different workflows"; "In HF-X data, DIA-NN with the project-specific DDA library under the regular cutoff yielded the largest number of up-regulated sites (183 sites)"; "In TIMS data, Spectronaut with the directDIA library under the regular cutoff yielded the highest number (254 sites)"; "smaller variations of phosphosite quantification were observed for HF data analysis by DIA-NN than Spectronaut" — [ResearchGate Fig. 5 record](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Comparison-of-DIA-NN-and-Spectronaut-in-TNF-a-induced-phosphoproteome-data-analysis-a_fig5_366927006); [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35740-1)
- Phosphosite localisation (review excerpt): "the DIA-NN localization algorithm was more conservative than Spectronaut's but resulted in lower sensitivity" — [MCP survey 2023](https://www.mcponline.org/article/S1535-9476(24)00002-1/pdf)
- Headline conclusion (abstract, excerpt): the study reports "distinct advantages of DIA-NN and Spectronaut in analyzing different types of DIA proteomics data acquired on state-of-the-art instruments" — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35740-1)
- Differential-expression threshold: a search summary stated that the design "targets the sensitivity to call differentially expressed proteins at a commonly applied 1.5-fold threshold"; the sentence could not be attributed to a specific page and may originate from a vendor note citing the paper — [Biognosys app note (vendor)](https://biognosys.com/content/uploads/2024/11/202410_Biognosys_Appnote_SN-TIMSTOF_A4_screen.pdf); [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35740-1)
- Third-party characterisation (review excerpt): "for the Orbitrap data, a universal library generated by FragPipe led to comparable murine protein identifications between DIA-NN, Skyline, and Spectronaut" — [PMC11380018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11380018/)

### Inferences
- The retrievable comparisons are per-tool performance against ground truth (expected ratios, known TNF-α sites, FDR), with "comparable" used for identification coverage, not for fold-change agreement between tools.
- "Regular cutoff" in Fig. 5 implies a defined significance/fold-change cut-off for regulated phosphosites; its value was not retrievable.

### Gaps

- Quantitative accuracy numbers (deviation of observed from expected log2 ratios), CV values, false-positive counts among yeast background proteins: not found.

## Key question 4 — Staes et al. (bioRxiv 2023 "Benchmarking DIA data analysis workflows", DOI 10.1101/2023.06.02.543441; journal version reported as "Benefit of In Silico Predicted Spectral Libraries in Data-Independent Acquisition Data Analysis Workflows", J Proteome Res 2024, 23(6):2078–2089, DOI 10.1021/acs.jproteome.4c00048) and Van Puyvelde et al. 2022, Sci Data 9:126, DOI 10.1038/s41597-022-01216-6

### Takeaway
The Staes preprint benchmarks 12 workflows (DIA-NN, EncyclopeDIA, Spectronaut; library and library-free) on a UPS2-in-yeast spike-in and concludes DIA-NN (library-free / predicted library) and Spectronaut (library-free) are most sensitive with "comparable" accuracy across workflows; no pairwise tool-tool agreement value was retrievable. Van Puyvelde 2022 is a dataset paper (>700 LC-MS runs, PXD028735), not a software comparison.

### Cited Findings
- Staes preprint authors and design (excerpt): An Staes, Teresa Maia, Sara Dufour, Robbin Bouwmeester, Ralf Gabriels, Lennart Martens, Francis Impens, Simon Devos; "a ground-truth sample, consisting of a differential spike-in of UPS2 in a constant yeast background"; "DIA-NN, EncyclopeDIA, and Spectronaut in both library and library-free modes"; "In total ... 12 DIA workflows" — [bioRxiv v1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.06.02.543441v1); [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.06.02.543441v1.full.pdf)
- Conclusion sentence (abstract, excerpt): "DIA-NN in library-free mode or using in silico predicted libraries, together with Spectronaut in library-free mode, shows the highest sensitivity while maintaining high reproducibility and accuracy" — [bioRxiv v1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.06.02.543441v1)
- Conclusion sentence (excerpt): "DIA-NN shows the best reproducibility, while the accuracy is comparable for all DIA workflows." — [bioRxiv v1 full](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.06.02.543441v1.full)
- Stated aim (excerpt): "a roadmap for DIA data analysis of shotgun samples based on whether sensitivity, precision or accuracy is of the essence" — [bioRxiv v1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.06.02.543441v1)
- Journal version (excerpt of abstract): the authors "evaluated the sensitivity, precision, and accuracy of in silico predicted libraries against more established workflows"; UPS2 "contains 48 human proteins with a dynamic range spanning six orders of magnitude" — [ACS JPR 4c00048](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jproteome.4c00048) (identity of this JPR article as the published Staes paper is a search-engine inference; author list of the journal version not confirmed)
- Van Puyvelde 2022 (excerpts): "A comprehensive LFQ benchmark dataset on modern day acquisition strategies in proteomics"; "The dataset consists of over 700 LC-MS runs, including adequate replicates allowing robust statistics and covering over nearly 10 different data formats, including scanning quadrupole and ion mobility enabled acquisitions"; "It contains a ground truth that serves as a quality control for bioinformatics algorithm development and evaluation"; PXD028735; design adapted from Navarro et al. — [PMC8967878](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8967878); [Nature Sci Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01216-6)

- Van Puyvelde 2022 supplies ground truth for others to compute agreement; it does not itself report software agreement metrics.

## Key question 5 — Navarro et al. 2016, Nat Biotechnol 34:1130–1136, DOI 10.1038/nbt.3685 ("A multicenter study benchmarks software tools for label-free proteome quantification"; LFQbench)

### Takeaway
Five SWATH tools (OpenSWATH, SWATH 2.0, Skyline, Spectronaut, DIA-Umpire) were scored with the LFQbench R package on hybrid-proteome samples of known composition (human/yeast/E. coli; HYE datasets) by precision and accuracy of log-ratios against expected values and by numbers of quantified peptides/proteins; the paper's own conclusion is that "after optimization, all tools provided highly convergent identification and reliable quantification performance". Numerical values and any tool-vs-tool correlation were not retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- Tools (abstract, excerpt): "OpenSWATH, SWATH 2.0, Skyline, Spectronaut and DIA-Umpire, five of the most widely used software methods for processing data from SWATH-MS"; developed "in collaboration with the developers" — [PubMed 27701404](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27701404/); [PMC5120688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5120688)

- Metrics (abstract, excerpt): LFQbench, "an R package" to "calculate metrics of precision and accuracy in label-free quantitative MS" — [PubMed 27701404](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27701404/); [GitHub IFIproteomics/LFQbench](https://github.com/IFIproteomics/LFQbench)

## Key question 6 — Kuharev et al. 2015, Proteomics 15(18):3140–3151, DOI 10.1002/pmic.201400396; Chawade et al. 2015, J Proteome Res 14(2):676–687, DOI 10.1021/pr500665j

### Takeaway
Kuharev 2015 compared Progenesis, synapter and ISOQuant on ion-mobility DIA (MS^E/HDMS^E) hybrid-proteome data by CV and accuracy against known ratios (median CVs < 5% for two tools); Chawade 2015 processed spiked DDA/SRM dilution series through eight workflows and concluded software choice "play[s] an important role in the final outcome". Neither paper's between-software fold-change correlation or direction agreement was retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- Kuharev tools and design (abstract, excerpt): "three different software packages (Progenesis, synapter, and ISOQuant) supporting ion mobility enhanced data-independent acquisition data"; "two hybrid proteome samples of defined quantitative composition containing tryptically digested proteomes of three different species (mouse, yeast, Escherichia coli)" — [Wiley Proteomics](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pmic.201400396)
- Kuharev precision (abstract, excerpt): "median coefficients of variation of reported protein abundances below 5% for MS^E data for Progenesis and ISOQuant" — [Wiley Proteomics](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pmic.201400396)
- Kuharev accuracy (abstract, excerpt): "evaluation with synapter and ISOQuant yielded superior results compared to Progenesis" — [Wiley Proteomics](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pmic.201400396)
- Kuharev also "examined the influence of algorithms such as retention time alignment and normalization" and introduced LFQbench as "an open source R package for the automated evaluation of label-free quantification performance" (excerpts) — [Wiley Proteomics](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pmic.201400396)
- Chawade design (abstract, excerpt): two benchmark datasets (DDA and SRM) from "a dilution series of synthetic peptides spiked into a total cell protein digest"; "processed by eight different software workflows and three postprocessing steps" — [PubMed 25407311](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25407311/)
- Chawade conclusion (abstract, excerpt): "the choice of the raw data processing software and the postprocessing steps play an important role in the final outcome" — [PubMed 25407311](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25407311/); [ACS JPR](https://pubs.acs.org/jprobs/article-abstract/14/2/676/1464141/Data-Processing-Has-Major-Impact-on-the-Outcome-of?redirectedFrom=fulltext)

### Inferences
- Both 2015 papers use ground-truth spike-in designs; agreement is expressed as per-tool CV and accuracy, consistent with the later LFQbench tradition.

### Gaps

## Key question 7 — Any paper reporting sign concordance / direction agreement (or other pairwise agreement) between two proteomics pipelines, with values

### Takeaway
No paper reporting an explicit "sign concordance" or "direction agreement" percentage between two DIA pipelines was found. The closest retrievable pairwise-agreement numbers are: a 2026 ACS Omega Spectronaut-vs-DIA-NN lung adenocarcinoma study (7180 shared proteins, 1130 shared DEPs, 34/50 shared top-50 DEPs), a TraianProt reanalysis (1349/2196 = 61% shared DE proteins, fold changes "highly concordant"), and a 2021 timsTOF preprint with inter-workflow correlations of roughly 0.85–0.89 for DIA-NN vs Spectronaut (quantity correlated not confirmed).

### Inferences
- In the retrievable literature, pipeline-vs-pipeline agreement is reported almost exclusively as (a) counts/percentages of shared identifications or shared significant proteins and (b) correlation of quantities, while "direction concordance" of significant proteins is not a reported metric; benchmark papers prefer ground-truth sensitivity/accuracy over pairwise concordance.
- None of the retrieved papers states a numeric agreement value as a pass/fail acceptance threshold; thresholds that appear are identification-level (1% FDR) or effect-size/significance cut-offs for calling DE (e.g., a 1.5-fold cut-off mentioned in connection with Lou 2023), not agreement cut-offs.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports a sign-concordance / direction-agreement percentage between two DIA pipelines (searched: "sign concordance", "direction of change", "same direction" with DIA-NN/Spectronaut/MaxQuant/FragPipe).
- For the ACS Omega 2026 LUAD paper: Pearson/Spearman of log2FC, how direction was scored, CVs, and the DE thresholds: not found.
- For the TraianProt and TIMS-PASEF preprints: exact correlation coefficients and what quantity they refer to: unverified / not found.
- No retrieved paper uses a pairwise agreement value as an explicit pass/fail threshold.
